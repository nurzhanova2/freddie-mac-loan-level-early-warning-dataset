# Diagnostic assessment of the academic revision

## Initial assessment

The chapters contained an adequate substantive basis for a dissertation: they
specified the sample, outcomes, temporal split, model results, and
interpretation limits. The earlier wording nevertheless sometimes resembled
technical documentation. Typical signals were “Status” sections and stage
references, emphasis on files and operations rather than methodological
rationale, future-tense statements after experiments had been completed, and
inconsistent terminology.

## Revision approach

The revision reorganised the material around the sequence research problem,
methodological requirement, chosen procedure, observed result, and boundary of
inference. Numerical findings, the Brier-score formula, outcome definitions,
and links to reproducible tables and figures were retained. Technical detail was
kept where it is necessary for reproducibility, leakage control, or result
interpretation.

## Elements to address in the final thesis version

- **[SOURCE INTEGRATION IN PROGRESS]** A working source register and in-text
  citations in Chapters 1–6 have been added. Before the final version is
  issued, the full reference list should be formatted to the university's
  required bibliographic standard and checked against the institutional template.
- **[JUSTIFICATION NEEDED]** If new models or cohorts are added, the comparison
  criterion and leading-model selection rule should be specified in advance.
- **[LIMITATION TO RETAIN]** Generalisation to Freddie Mac, other populations,
  or future periods requires independent validation.
- **[INTERPRETATION BOUNDARY]** SHAP values and observational associations
  should not be written as causal effects or as evidence of an intervention's
  supervisory benefit.
